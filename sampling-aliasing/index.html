The rule that saves you

The sampling theorem

If a signal is band-limited to fmax, sample it at a rate greater than twice that frequency and the original can be perfectly reconstructed.

fs > 2fmax

The boundary, fs/2, is the Nyquist frequency for a given sampler.

The failure mode

Aliasing is a frequency fold.

When the sample rate is too low, the sampler cannot distinguish between different fast waves. Their samples land in the same places, so the reconstruction chooses the wrong identity.

“

Two different continuous signals can produce the exact same sequence of samples.

That false, lower-frequency look-alike is called an alias. It is not noise; it is a perfectly consistent — and perfectly wrong — interpretation of the data.
